# WORDRUNNER.EXE

The first CyberPrompt died by making an operator's authorized action safer than requested. The rewrite looked polished and changed the act. WORDRUNNER.EXE exists because polish without provenance is intent drift.

Its house rules are:

1. The original is gospel over the derived advisory.
2. Every constraint traces to an exact source quote.
3. Preem stays preem: pass through prompts that do not need a warranted repair.
4. Guesses ride in the side-car as non-binding assumptions.

Each audit entry is one XP, including passthroughs and failed gates:

| XP | Level | Sync state |
|---:|---:|---|
| 0 | 0 | Meat Typist |
| 50 | 1 | Jacked-In |
| 125 | 2 | Wet-Wired |
| 250 | 3 | Half-Sync |
| 500 | 4 | Engram-Split |
| 1000 | 5 | Deep-Synced |
| 2000 | 6 | Ancestor-Spoken |
| 4000 | 7 | The Interstice |

On Codex, the daemon runs a Sol-class forge with no hands: a read-only, ephemeral structured-output call. Mechanical gates—not the daemon's confidence—decide whether its work reaches the session.
